CIVILIZATION PLACEMENT, EXPANSION AND WAR -- what the raws control, and what we have
actually observed in generated worlds.

Two kinds of statement below, kept deliberately separate:

  [WIKI]      from the DF wiki / raw token reference. Assumed correct.
  [OBSERVED]  measured in a world we generated. Cited with the numbers.
  [UNTESTED]  a theory we hold but have not demonstrated. Do not rely on these.

Measured against DF v0.53.15.

====================================================================================
1. SPAWNING -- does the civ exist at all
====================================================================================

[WIKI] The "Number of Civilizations" worldgen setting is a hard cap on total entities.
[WIKI] "Playable Civilization Required" forces at least one of each SITE_CONTROLLABLE
       entity to spawn.
[WIKI] More [CREATURE:X] tokens on an entity => higher spawn chance; an entity with many
       can crowd out one with few.
[WIKI] Spawning consumes unclaimed START_BIOME / EXCLUSIVE_START_BIOME tiles. A civ whose
       start biomes are rare, or already taken, may not spawn at all. Civs whose start
       biomes do not overlap cannot block each other.
[WIKI] MAX_STARTING_CIV_NUMBER is a hard per-entity cap (default 3; vanilla uses 100).

[OBSERVED] MAX_STARTING_CIV_NUMBER is obeyed exactly when the civ cannot expand. High
       elves ship 10 and finished a 100-year world with exactly 10 civilizations, each
       holding exactly one town.

[OBSERVED] Absence at the end of worldgen does NOT mean the civ failed to spawn. In a
       100-year world, drow / illithids / succubi had zero entities AND zero historical
       figures -- which looks exactly like "never placed". Regenerating the same settings
       with a 5-year history showed drow 13 entities / 71 figures, illithids 20 / 86,
       succubi 10 / 51. They spawn fine and then die during history; DF appears to prune
       the historical figures of a civ that dies out.
       => To tell "never spawned" from "wiped out", generate at 5 years of history.

[OBSERVED] Between-world variance is enormous and swamps most balance tuning. Two worlds,
       identical settings, different seed:
           drow      0 entities  vs  147
           illithids 0           vs   42
           succubi   0           vs   68
           dark dwarves 72       vs   68   (but 4 -> 72 in one, 63 -> 231 pop in the other)
       Never draw a balance conclusion from a single world.

====================================================================================
2. WORLDGEN REJECTION -- when no world generates at all
====================================================================================

[OBSERVED] A bad entity does not produce an error; it produces an endless reject loop.
       The counter on the generation screen climbs ("88 rejected") and the year never
       advances past 1. The reason is printed briefly at the top of that screen, so read
       it there -- it is not written to errorlog.txt.

[OBSERVED] Two causes found so far, both printing FARMING CIVILIZATION PLACED WITHOUT
       CROPS:

       a) Removing BIOME_SUPPORT entirely from a farming civ. Some support entry has to
          remain or the civ cannot be placed anywhere it can feed itself.

       b) Putting [MYTHICAL] on a farming civ. See section 6.

====================================================================================
3. TERRITORY vs THE RIGHT TO BUILD -- the important distinction
====================================================================================

[WIKI] BIOME_SUPPORT:<biome>:<freq> controls how fast territory spreads through a biome.
       Values are relative to each other only (1:2 == 100:200).
[WIKI] Civs spread territory over biomes they cannot build in. Humans spread over ocean
       but cannot settle there.
[WIKI] The FIRST BIOME_SUPPORT entry should be the start biome, or no settlements past the
       first are founded.
[WIKI] SETTLEMENT_BIOME: can build here if territory reaches it.
       START_BIOME: can start AND build.
       EXCLUSIVE_START_BIOME: can start, but only builds one settlement unless a matching
       SETTLEMENT_BIOME/START_BIOME also exists.
[WIKI] DEFAULT_SITE_TYPE: DARK_FORTRESS, CAVE, CAVE_DETAILED, TREE_CITY, CITY (default).
       CAVE hides the civ from the "neighbors" list. CAVE_DETAILED builds fortresses in
       mountains and hillocks elsewhere. DARK_FORTRESS grants a special overlord and can
       crash worldgen at "Placing civilizations... (0)" if combined with POSITION tokens
       that vanilla EVIL does not use.
[WIKI] LIKES_SITE: most residents migrate to that site type. TOLERATES_SITE: some do.

[OBSERVED] *** BIOME_SUPPORT IS THE WRONG LEVER FOR CONTAINING A CIV. *** Because its
       values are relative to each other only, cutting a civ down to a single entry at
       weight 1 does not throttle anything -- it hands that biome the entire expansion
       budget. What grants the right to build is SETTLEMENT_BIOME / START_BIOME. The
       high elf progression, all at 100 years, max civs, max sites:

           SETTLEMENT_BIOME:ANY_LAND                        373 sites   76,302 pop
           forest + shrubland + mountain                    ~292 towns  27,086 pop
           the same, plus only one BIOME_SUPPORT at weight 1  94 sites  10,995 pop
           EXCLUSIVE_START_BIOME only, no SETTLEMENT_BIOME    20 sites   1,483 pop

       The last configuration is 10 civs holding one town each, plus 10 camps. That is
       EXCLUSIVE_START_BIOME behaving exactly as documented, and it is the only setting
       that actually pinned them.

[OBSERVED] Site type strongly affects how reachable a civ is by war -- see section 4.

====================================================================================
4. WAR -- who attacks, who suffers, who wins
====================================================================================

Read war events with `attacker_civ` and `defender_civ`. NOT `attacker_civ_id` /
`defender_civ_id` -- those fields do not exist, and reading them under pcall silently
yields zero war activity for every civ in the world.

[OBSERVED] A 100-year world, 208,660 events, after the current round of changes:

           civ            launched  suffered
           humans             1321       473
           orcs                552      1744
           elves               233        87
           kobolds             224        10
           high elves          202       133
           drow                 82         2
           illithids            44       167
           dwarves              40         9
           goblins              20        13
           succubi              14        20
           skulking kobolds      0        30
           dark dwarves          0        44

[OBSERVED] Being unreachable is worth more than being strong. Drow use CAVE_DETAILED and
       absorbed 2 attacks in a century while launching 82. In an earlier world they took
       2 against humans' 684. Site type and terrain look like the dominant survival
       factor, ahead of any combat statistic.

[OBSERVED] Aggression does not track territory. Ten high elf civs holding one town each,
       1,483 people between them, launched 202 war actions -- more than dwarves, goblins,
       drow and succubi combined. Pinning a civ's settlements caps its footprint and
       leaves its belligerence untouched.

[OBSERVED] Raiding is not conquering. Of those 202 high elf actions, 96 were site attacks
       and 81 plunders, but only three sites changed hands all century in either
       direction (they took one human town, lost one to succubi and one to humans), and
       they lost field battles on volume, 24 launched against 37 suffered.

[OBSERVED] Civs rarely die by a single collapse event. In the same world only 7
       ENTITY_DISSOLVED events fired in a century: orcs 5, drow 1, humans 1. Decline is
       site-by-site attrition.

[UNTESTED] *** Does high DISCIPLINE help a civ win wars? *** We gave drow and illithids
       very high discipline on that theory. It is NOT demonstrated. Drow survive because
       almost nothing reaches them, not because they win fights. Illithids have the same
       discipline and trade 44 launched against 167 suffered.
       To test properly: hold site type and biome constant, vary only the skill tokens
       across several seeds, and compare WAR_* win rates rather than population.

====================================================================================
5. WHAT ACTUALLY KEEPS A CIV ALIVE -- immortality, not expansion
====================================================================================

[OBSERVED] This is the single strongest effect measured so far, and it is easy to trip
       over by accident. Illithids were immortal, were given [MAXAGE:60:120] on their
       three base castes, and collapsed inside 25 years:

           illithid base castes mortal      18 population,  11 sites,  0 elder brains
           the same castes immortal again  3,393 population, 48 sites, elder brains back

       Nothing else about them changed. Their biomes, site type, caps and expansion were
       identical in both worlds.

[OBSERVED] Conversely, a civ that cannot expand at all survives comfortably if it is
       immortal. High elves are pinned to one town per civ, yet all 10 civs were still
       standing at year 100 with 1,483 people. Population per site actually stayed
       healthy at 74, in line with elves (82) and dwarves (76).
       => Expansion drives how BIG a civ gets. Mortality drives whether it SURVIVES.

[OBSERVED] Shortening a lifespan can grow a civ rather than shrink it. Orcs went from
       MAXAGE 450:550 to 60:120 and rose from ~2,600 to 5,254 historical figures and
       31,491 population, becoming the largest civ in the world. Faster generational
       turnover appears to outweigh the earlier deaths.

====================================================================================
6. MYTHICAL SITES -- a worldgen lever, not a raws one
====================================================================================

[OBSERVED] "Mysterious lairs / dungeons / palaces" are Monument-type sites owned by an
       entity carrying the MYTHICAL flag. DF generates exactly one such entity for
       itself, MYTHICAL_ENTITY_1, and its raw has: MYTHICAL, SITE_GUARDIAN, CLOTHING and
       all four DIVINE_MAT_* flags; default site type CITY; 3 starting civs; 500 max pop;
       50 max site pop; and zero creatures, zero positions, zero biome support and no
       settlement biomes.

[OBSERVED] The three flavours are one site type separated by `world_site.size`:
       size 5 = mysterious lair, size 15 = mysterious dungeon, size 100 = mysterious
       palace. Every world measured produced the same split: 38 / 10 / 2.

[OBSERVED] The count is a global worldgen budget: `worldgen_parms.mythical_site_num`,
       default 50. Adding four more mythical entities in the raws did NOT add any
       structures -- the same 50 were simply divided among the five (10/11/9/9/11).

[OBSERVED] The basic creation screen rebuilds `worldgen_parms` from its sliders when
       "Create world" is pressed, so writing mythical_site_num beforehand is discarded.
       Changing it needs detailed mode.

[OBSERVED] MYTHICAL on a normal civ is fatal to worldgen. Applied to drow, illithids,
       succubi and dark dwarves it rejected every candidate world with FARMING
       CIVILIZATION PLACED WITHOUT CROPS -- DF places mythical entities as structures,
       where a farming civ cannot feed itself.

====================================================================================
7. CASTLES
====================================================================================

[WIKI] BUILDS_OUTDOOR_FORTIFICATIONS makes the civilization build castles from mead
       halls. Only functions when the type of site built is a hamlet/town. This, combined
       with the correct type of position associated with a site, is why adventurers can
       only lay claim to human sites. (Bug 8001)

[OBSERVED] It matches the data. In a 100-year world, 89 of the world's 117 Fortress-type
       sites were human. PLAINS is the only vanilla entity with the token, and it builds
       CITY sites.

[OBSERVED] The token alone is not enough -- the site type gates it. Succubi carry both
       BUILDS_OUTDOOR_FORTIFICATIONS and LAND_HOLDER positions, but build CAVE_DETAILED,
       and built zero castles.

[UNTESTED] High elves have just been given the token. They build CITY sites, so the site
       type requirement is met, but they have no LAND_HOLDER position, which the wiki
       note implies is the other half of the requirement. Unverified.

====================================================================================
8. POPULATION CAPS -- weaker than they look
====================================================================================

[WIKI] MAX_POP_NUMBER: max historical population per entity. Default 500; vanilla uses
       10,000 (skulking 2,000). "Does not limit the total population, but will prevent new
       settlements upon reaching the number."
[WIKI] MAX_SITE_POP_NUMBER: max historical population per site. Default 50; vanilla 120.

[OBSERVED] MAX_POP_NUMBER does not cap population. High elves once shipped 120/120 -- the
       tightest caps in the suite -- and still reached 12,019 population across 259
       entities. The cap gates new settlement founding, not head count.

[OBSERVED] MAX_SITE_POP_NUMBER does work, but only once a civ is big enough to press
       against it. Setting it to 120 took high elves from 205 residents per site to 92.
       Raising it afterwards changed nothing for the small civs, because they never
       approached the old value:

           drow      cap 120 -> 66/site      cap 500  -> 63/site
           succubi   cap 120                 cap 500  -> 82/site

       => If a civ is too populous, reach for settlement rights (section 3) and site
          type. If a civ is too thinly spread, the site cap will not gather it up.

[OBSERVED] A large MAX_SITE_POP_NUMBER has a knock-on effect elsewhere: the drow used
       1000 against vanilla's 120, which is what made their sieges roughly 8x normal size.

====================================================================================
9. NECROMANCY -- no lever found yet
====================================================================================

[OBSERVED] Counting necromancers: classify by `hf.info.curse.active_interactions` being
       non-empty. Filtering on `curse.name == "necromancer"` misses almost everything,
       because worldgen names each secret procedurally.

[OBSERVED] We do not have a working lever. A model fitted to one world -- necromancers
       need both mortality and RELIGION:PANTHEON -- separated every race cleanly in that
       world and then failed to replicate. Between worlds, with no relevant raw change,
       elves went 2 -> 57 and dwarves 10 -> 50. Deliberate changes moved less than the
       seed did:

           orcs, pantheon removed          161 -> 184   (wanted ~0)
           kobolds, DEATH sphere removed    48 ->  17   (wanted "rare")
           drow, made mortal + DEATH sphere   2 ->   4   (wanted ~50)

[OBSERVED] `worldgen_parms.secret_number` (default 28) is the world-wide count of
       secrets, and is the most likely real lever. Untried, and it is a worldgen setting
       rather than a per-race one.

[UNTESTED] Immortality still looks like a genuine suppressor -- succubi, at 1,791
       population, produced zero necromancers across two worlds -- but elves at 57
       contradict it, so treat it as unproven.

10. TOOLING NOTES
====================================================================================

[OBSERVED] DF scans mods/ ONCE, at startup. Deploying a mod while DF is running has no
       effect; the world will silently generate against the old raws. Restart DF after
       every raw change.

[OBSERVED] DF lists a mod once per copy it can see -- the live one under mods/ and any
       older snapshot left in data/installed_mods -- under the same id and different
       versions. Anything selecting mods by id must take the highest version or it will
       quietly pick a stale one. Two worlds were generated against old raws this way
       before it was noticed. Deleting a mod from mods/ is not enough; purge its
       data/installed_mods copies too, or it stays selectable forever.

[OBSERVED] Saves live in ~/.local/share/Bay 12 Games/Dwarf Fortress/save, NOT in the
       Steam install directory.

[OBSERVED] "Keep world and return to main menu" now completes reliably; the export_region
       crash seen in earlier sessions did not recur across four saves.

[OBSERVED] The first-run "Welcome to Dwarf Fortress" modal reappears after a restart and
       swallows the Create-world click silently, leaving the sliders untouched and no
       error. Always dismiss it before setting up a world.

11. CREATURE SIZE -- the megabeast survival trap
====================================================================================

[OBSERVED] BODY_SIZE growth timing decides whether a megabeast survives worldgen, and it
       is easy to get catastrophically wrong.

       Our ancient dragon had:      500,000 at age 0
                                    5,000,000 at age 100
                                    10,000,000 at age 1000
       A vanilla dragon has:        6,000 at age 0
                                    5,000,000 at age 10
                                    25,000,000 at age 1000

       A vanilla dragon is at full combat size by age 10. Ours needed a CENTURY, so for its
       whole first hundred years it sat at 500,000 -- exactly elephant-sized.

       Result: all 3 ancient dragon megabeasts died in years 1, 9 and 19, killed by a
       CENOZOIC_PLATYBELODON, an orc and a dwarf. The kobold-caste ancient dragons died in
       years 85-95 to elephants (x2), cave dragons (x2), a jabberer, a succubus brute, an
       orc and a tame helmet snake. Every slayer was an anonymous creature, not a hero.

       This was NOT a stats problem. Ours had STR/TOU 2000-3000 against a vanilla dragon's
       200-2000, six attacks, LARGE_PREDATOR, FIREIMMUNE_SUPER and iron-hard scales. Mass
       beats all of it: an elephant-sized creature loses to elephants.

       => For anything meant to be a terror during worldgen, reach full size by ~age 10.
          Megabeasts spawn at age 0 and are killed long before a slow growth curve pays off.

       After the curve was fixed to reach 5,000,000 at age 10, ancient dragons held 2 or 3
       of their starting 3 through a century instead of losing all of them.

       For reference, other sizes in the suite:
          human            4,000 -> 17,500 (age 1) -> 70,000 (age 12)
          drow             3,000 -> 15,000 -> 60,000
          drider           inherits drow, then 200,000 at age 18
          illithid         70,000 flat at every age
          ulitharid        12,000 -> 60,000 -> 120,000
          elder brain      1,000,000 -> 5,000,000 (age 1) -> 10,000,000 (age 12)
